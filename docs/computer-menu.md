# Computer menu: first playable version

The main menu has an original procedural 3D computer, a live CRT-style screen and a floating carousel of saved decks beneath it. This is a working version of the computer/deck-drive concept; the hardware materials and animation timing can be refined without changing the Forge backend.

## Molded hardware controls

PLAY and SETTINGS occupy actual recessed openings in the lower fascia, aligned on the floppy slot's center line. Both keycaps use the same height, depth, small corner radius and crisp chamfer. Their cool-gray plastic matches the case's color, roughness and fine grain, with separate material instances for hover and press feedback. Dark well walls and a soft baked contact gradient shade the narrow seam. The flat cap tops sit just proud of the case, with room to move into the pockets.

The solid triangle, chunky six-tooth gear and small tracked condensed PLAY legend are charcoal pad-printing with no glow. Only the plastic brightens slightly on hover. Mouse-down moves the cap inward by 0.028 scene units over 60 ms and visibly darkens its face. The cap stays depressed while held, then springs outward immediately over 100 ms on release with a small outward spring. Quick taps finish only the 60 ms downstroke; there is no extra depressed dwell or inward dip on release. Dragging off cancels the action and releases the key. Both actions wait for the current release tween and one fully raised rendered frame before image preparation, settings construction or camera/disk movement. The waiter follows tween replacement on mouse-up, and launch consumes the existing native press rather than replaying it. Busy press phases protect that feedback from background deck refreshes. A tiny teal dot on PLAY lights for that brief press and is otherwise unlit. The existing native hit regions, validation, launch sequence and settings-camera callbacks remain in use.

## Use it

```bash
bash ./client-godot/tools/run.sh
```

After the startup screen finishes, the monitor shows a teal-on-black Commander terminal with the selected deck, format/opponents and validation status. The previous Commander/Deck Editor screen buttons are removed; Deck Editor remains in the outer header. Select a neighboring floppy below the computer; the monitor displays that disk's information without a second selector. Decks appear as sideways colored floppy disks, with a metal shutter on the left and the exact-printing deck image on an inset paper label on the right. The selected floppy is larger, raised, gently bobbing and rocking beneath the computer; neighbors sit lower and smaller with angled faces and shadows. The carousel has no separate footer name or navigation arrows. Click a neighbor, drag horizontally, swipe on a trackpad, scroll the mouse wheel, or use Left/Right after focusing the carousel. Selection flows smoothly between disks, preserving position when reversing direction. Libraries of four or more decks wrap around; smaller libraries stop at their ends to avoid visible side jumps. The selected disk is larger than the initial carousel, and the computer uses closer framing for legibility.

Click the physical **Play** key to the left of the floppy slot. The key smoothly depresses and releases before the floppy lifts; it stays disabled while loading or when the selected deck is invalid. The selected floppy starts at its current position and tilt, rotates label-up with its metal shutter toward the drive, aligns with the narrow mouth, then slides into the case. Its starting depth is calculated from its carousel size so it keeps a fixed physical size while traveling toward the drive. The same rendered face and shell color carry from the carousel onto the physical 3D disk. The game engine starts in parallel. The drive indicator pulses and the teal terminal shows loading feedback until Forge is ready. The camera then approaches the monitor while the actual game is displayed inside it; the screen expands to the window's aspect ratio before taking over the whole window.

The full game uses the same game scene and live adapter as before. During the zoom its input is blocked. After the zoom it is reparented out of the temporary render viewport and receives normal full-window input. Its existing starting-player/opening-hand prompts remain authoritative. No game state is restarted by the visual transition.

A thin rounded frame surrounds the menu, with compact controls interrupting its top edge: a FORGE connection badge on the left, Deck Editor centered and Quit on the right. The badge star turns blue while the library WebSocket is connected and muted gray when disconnected; its tooltip explains the state. Teal pigment and fine speckles spread broadly around the PC with softer edges. Left/Right navigate the carousel from the main menu. Enter triggers the same validated physical Play sequence. Returning home keeps the match alive: the camera pulls back, the deck floppy ejects into the carousel, and a second disk with live-match information ejects to the side. Click that session disk to insert it and zoom back into the same table. End Game is a separate confirmed action. Carousel motion is frozen during insertion/ejection so the landing matches. Unsaved editor drafts remain cached. The adjacent settings key opens the terminal roster for bot decks, names and portraits. No-deck and invalid-deck states keep Start disabled and direct users to the editor/Forge validation. See [live session disks](live-session-disk.md) for retention, background connection and cleanup behavior.

## Implementation

The read-only deck console shares the initial ForgeCheck diagnostic palette. It shows DISK, commander, format/player count, Commander color identity, average nonland mana value, an ASCII mana curve (0 through 7+), card-type counts, legality and a segmented card-count meter. All figures come from the selected deck's Main and Commander sections; multitype cards count toward each applicable type. The floppy carousel is the sole deck selector. There are no prompts or parked-game messages below the meter; the separate session disk still resumes a live match.

DeckConsole.gd draws within a 64 px horizontal / 48 px vertical safe inset in the 1024x744 display, keeping labels and the meter away from rounded glass edges. Long disk names are shortened, commander names use at most two lines, secondary labels are brighter, and the camera frames the computer about 12% larger. The room scroll clears that larger computer completely when opening the editor. The glass adds a restrained phosphor halo, faint scanlines, a corner vignette and a broad soft reflection. These effects fade during fullscreen handoff rather than affecting the game UI.

Switching between Home and Deck Editor keeps the common visible disks in place as the room scrolls. Wrapped edge disks slide and fade away when entering the finite editor; newly visible home slots fade back in on return. The blank + disk follows the same transition. These temporary edge images share the existing floppy textures and are removed when the animation ends.

Right-click a floppy and choose **Pick color from card…** or **Custom color…** to open the color picker beside its card image. Hover the card to preview an exact source-image shade and hex code, click to select it, then **Apply color** to save. Closing without applying preserves the floppy's previous color. Sampling ignores the room lighting and the floppy's surface shading.

- `scripts/app/ComputerWorld.gd`: beveled procedural case, bezel, screen, drive, light, studio setup, physical card stack, insertion tween and camera zoom. No external model or bitmap of the computer is required.
- `scripts/app/ComputerMenu.gd`: world/display viewports, monitor UI, ray-to-screen input mapping, loading and transition coordination. Hidden menu viewports stop rendering while in the editor/game.
- `scripts/app/DeckConsole.gd`: read-only terminal layout, deck statistics and rounded-glass safe area.
- `scripts/app/DeckCarousel.gd`: continuous floating layout, depth/scale/tilt/bob and click/drag/wheel/trackpad/keyboard selection. Motion stops processing when hidden. Carousel selection updates the monitor's disk label, statistics and Forge deck validation.
- `scripts/app/DeckCartridge.gd` and `FloppyDisk.gdshader`: floppy face with deterministic shell color, metal shutter/window, clipped corner, molded seam/apertures and inset deck label. Each face renders once into a transparent 512-pixel viewport, updating when its art arrives; the same texture feeds the carousel and physical insertion. The face rectangle and tilt provide the animation's starting pose.
- `scripts/app/Application.gd`: existing service/session ownership plus asynchronous insertion/engine-readiness gating, temporary rendering of the real game onto the monitor, and native fullscreen handoff.

The hardware now follows the supplied compact Macintosh reference: a continuous flat front fascia, actual recessed openings, a sloped plastic screen reveal, separate black CRT gasket, and one continuous disk slot with a dark throat and recessed flap. The casing includes a raised top deck and moulded grooves. A longer camera lens reveals the top while reducing perspective distortion. The Apple badge has been removed at the user's request.

A broad bevel follows the full front frame, using multiple connected profiles and smooth corner normals to catch light. The front plastic, rear shell, bevel, rough inner recess, rubber gasket and metal drive parts have distinct finishes. Shared deterministic noise textures provide fine normal relief, restrained tonal variation and roughness variation with triplanar mapping and mipmaps. The materials require no downloaded texture assets.

Dark mode is the same room lit at night: lower general illumination, real teal CRT spill across the case/keys/drive/floor, a dim cool rear rim and a quiet radial background. Carousel disks use a separate presentation-only night shader with continuous center falloff; their raw face textures and sampled colors are unchanged. The insertion face blends into real scene lighting as it approaches the drive. Every change follows the existing room darkness tween and returns to the original materials/lights at zero. The home-to-editor scroll fades the new disk treatment out with the room. See [night-lighting values, file map and before/after captures](night-lighting.md).

The case palette and ambient lighting are neutral slate gray. The menu background and floor share a cool light gray (#d9dfe2), matching the reference's paper tone. `MenuAtmosphere.gdshader` applies a finish over the rendered computer and menu controls: softly scattered teal pigment, subdued static pixel grain and sparse teal flecks, following the supplied frosted/paper reference. An elliptical falloff concentrates the effect around the PC and leaves the screen edges almost clean. It uses screen-space noise with no external assets or animation. The overlay ignores mouse input, follows menu visibility, and fades its shader strength to zero during the camera transition into gameplay. Returning to the menu restores the finish.

Floppy shells use a fixed retro '80s palette: golden yellow, orange, coral, pink, lavender, cobalt, teal and mint. A stable hash of the deck ID chooses the default color, so artwork loading or changing the cover never recolors the plastic. The same shell color transfers to the physical disk during insertion/ejection. Deck-editor color customization is deferred.

The screen uses a spatial shader for rounded glass edges, restrained teal phosphor glow, a faint reflection, scanlines and a vignette. The terminal uses brighter monospace teal text on black with thin dividers. Those effects blend out as the camera zooms. The monitor's physical width adjusts during the game zoom so the final rendered game matches the actual window, avoiding a stretched fullscreen image. The initial desktop follows the light computer/studio reference; the deck editor and game keep their existing shared styling.

When the floppy reaches the drive opening and starts sliding in, MatchBootLog.gd prints an uppercase FORGECHECK boot log: the actual disk name, commander, verified Main+Commander card total, rules engine, player seats/names and library shuffle. Characters and dots appear first; OK lands a short beat later. Metadata lines use the already validated disk. Rules completion follows the real match-service ready signal. Seating uses authoritative snapshot names. Forge prepares/shuffles the libraries before its initial player/hand choice, so the last OK waits for a connected, populated snapshot with an available startup interaction rather than an empty or locked socket snapshot. That choice remains for the player after the transition. If Forge instead pauses during preparation for a required warning or other modal response, the final row becomes AWAITING PLAYER INPUT and opens that interaction without claiming the shuffle has finished. Session disks use CONNECTING LIVE ENGINE / RESTORING TABLE instead of claiming to start or reshuffle the existing game.

TerminalMeter.gd draws the same 50-segment phosphor blocks for deck-check and boot progress. Estimates ease within the active stage and cannot reach 100% until all readiness gates complete. Each printed chunk and OK pulses the physical drive LED; idle waiting no longer produces unrelated continuous drive activity. A blinking block cursor follows BOOTING COMMANDER. At completion, a restrained teal screen flash and READY. ENTERING TABLE... hold for 0.65 seconds at 100% before the table reveal. The selected-deck console remains on screen while the floppy approaches; the intermediate START COMMAND ACCEPTED / INSERTING DECK screen is removed. A ComputerWorld.disk_entering signal starts the log at drive contact for both new and resumed games. Image preparation and its cancel/retry flow still occur before insertion. A table connection that cannot become ready shows an error instead of completing on a timer.

The camera then stays at its home pose while a 1.1-second phosphor scan materializes the actual live table in small tiles, resolving coarse pixels into full detail. The completed picture settles for 0.18 seconds, then the existing camera zoom begins. The same game scene and viewport texture carry through all stages. App integration asserts real readiness, boot completion/payoff and stationary-camera materialization before zoom; `app-match-boot-rules.png`, `app-match-boot-ready.png`, `app-session-boot-log-1280.png` and `app-board-materializing.png` capture these stages.

The preview masks opening-hand, starting-player and decision overlays so the board/grid is what materializes. Main's transition_preview changes rendering opacity only; it preserves the actual modal visibility flags, callbacks, opening-hand state and engine decisions. The overlays are restored only after the game is reparented to the full-window scene. The battlefield hand itself is withheld during startup and mulligans, including View Battlefield and pending London bottom-card choices. OpeningHand.hand_confirmed releases it only after Forge accepts the final hand; reconnecting to an established game bypasses that opening gate. Masking runs on scene readiness, snapshots and pre-draw so asynchronous prompts cannot cover the cinematic. Toast fades continue independently and expired messages are not revived. Native/headless integration verifies masked prompts during materialization, restored prompts after zoom, and the normal opening-hand flow.

During zoom the display switches to a transparent-pass material with depth testing disabled. Its expanding edges can smoothly cover the physical bezel rather than remaining clipped behind it until the native game appears. At rest the normal depth-tested CRT material is restored. This fixes the side-edge pop in 16:9 and wider windows without changing the computer model's resting screen proportions.

This version has no custom sound effects or external high-detail computer model. Entering and leaving games both use the computer/disk transition.

## Verification

```powershell
node client-godot/tools/app-check.mjs --render
node client-godot/tools/app-check.mjs --render --menu-preview
node client-godot/tools/zoom-check.mjs
node client-godot/tools/app-check.mjs
node client-godot/tools/app-check.mjs --render --startup-failure

```

The real-service application integration uses isolated decks and verifies the read-only console, actual deck statistics, long-name bounds inside the rounded glass, physical key input, insertion/loading state, carousel neighbor clicks and dragging, selected-deck size/depth, wrapping, 1600×1000 and 1280×800 layout, fullscreen handoff, two/three/four-player Commander matches, menu/editor navigation and match cleanup. The fixture includes eighteen decks. Existing game smoke checks remain enabled.

Native captures include `app-main-menu.png`, `app-main-menu-1280.png`, `app-commander-setup.png`, `app-deck-insertion.png`, `app-monitor-zoom.png` and `app-commander-game.png` under `client-godot/artifacts/`.

For material/camera iteration, `--render --menu-preview` captures the menu at both sizes and checks native carousel clicks, drag selection, scale/depth and wrapping, then exits before launching games. Omit `--menu-preview` to run the full application integration.

`zoom-check.mjs` runs a native render regression without Forge. It compares a static calibration image immediately before and after the monitor-to-native handoff at 1600x1000, 1600x900 and 1920x800, including edge samples. Captures are `zoom-WIDTHxHEIGHT-before.png` and `-after.png`. The test reproduced the 16:9 clipping before the material fix and passes at all three aspect ratios after it.

The floppy shutter follows the supplied physical disk reference: metal folds over the leading edge, with rounded trailing corners and a narrow offset opening exposing the shell. A thin rim and contact shadow, shallow molded track, satin metal grain, shoulder notches and a centered embossed insertion arrow replace the previous thick metal frame. The shared face is used during insertion/ejection as well as in the carousel. `app-floppy-detail.png` provides a 512-pixel close-up from the rendered menu preview.

Default launch requests a 2560x1440 client area. WindowPlacement fits the complete window, including native title bar and borders, within the current monitor work area. When necessary it reduces the client size proportionally to retain 16:9 and keeps the window centered on that monitor. Explicit command-line placement and fullscreen/maximized launches are respected.

Looping carousel rows repeat edge slots using lightweight controls that share the original deck face texture. This keeps small collections continuous on wide windows: a disk no longer jumps across the visible row when its nearest wrapped position changes. Repeated slots select the same source deck, and queued arrow presses preserve direction while the animation catches up. The original centered disk remains the insertion/ejection source.

Initial application startup uses StartupScreen.gd's teal-on-black ForgeCheck diagnostic terminal, inspired by the supplied DOS utility reference. It has double-line borders, a highlighted section strip, configuration/status panels, a numbered service log and a 48-segment loading bar along the bottom. The eye, tracking box and snake square are no longer rendered. Startup-file stages and the actual WebSocket/deck-list response drive monotonic progress: runtime start (8%), rules ready (55%), catalog ready (82%), listener ready (90%), client connected (95%) and deck data received (100%). Elapsed time and a cursor animate while waiting; time alone never advances progress.

After deck data arrives, the bar completes and SYSTEM CHECK COMPLETE holds briefly. The same live terminal is reparented into an input-disabled SubViewport and pulled back into the physical PC screen over 1.65 seconds, retaining the fixed-glass projection. The monitor then tunes into the deck console over 0.70 seconds. Failures halt the bar, show the failed service/message and retain Escape/Quit. Match loading and its grid-only reveal remain separate. Old eye textures/shader are retained as unused prior assets.

Rendered app checks capture app-startup-loading.png, app-startup-verified.png and app-startup-error.png. They verify visible startup before the backend exists, animation during a stalled zero-percent stage, full progress before menu entry, the actual backend failure path and normal game navigation afterward.

Startup pullback continuity is checked against rendered fullscreen pixels at 1600x1000, 1600x900 and 1920x800 by zoom-check.mjs. Application captures app-startup-pullback.png and app-startup-on-monitor.png show the live verification screen during camera retreat and on the PC before tuning to the console. The existing game-return pullback keeps its 0.95-second duration.

The newer front-on hardware reference is reflected in a continuous molded screen reveal: a narrow rolled edge, steep plastic chamfer, wider bright lower bevel and a taller dark upper hood behind the glass. The floppy aperture is one continuous slot with a consistent 45-degree molded bevel, three-segment edge rolls, dark straight walls and a recessed flap. The former right-hand pocket and eject cap are removed; the fitted slot is centered between SETTINGS and the flat fascia's right edge. Its feed alignment follows the slot, while height and depth stay unchanged. The activity dot sits on the slot centerline in the right-hand margin. Disks complete their rotation clear of the fascia, then feed straight along the unchanged axis; the flap clears the shared disk during insertion/ejection. See [drive geometry, dimensions and before/after captures](drive-geometry.md).

The CRT now uses a subdivided 40x28 mesh. Its center bows forward by 0.065 world units and its outline has restrained tube curvature; dark backing prevents rear casing from appearing through the glass/surround seam. Glass reflections use a neutral dark edge instead of the previous teal rim. Curvature fades to zero with clean_view during fullscreen transitions, preserving pixel alignment. screen_point and iterative ray-based screen_uv use the same curved profile so on-monitor controls remain accurate. Rendered zoom checks verify projected control coordinates and fullscreen continuity at all three aspect ratios.

The glass/display now fills the monitor recess with a thin dark gasket rather than the earlier oversized upper surround. Display dimensions are 2.48x1.83 at y=1.30; backing, projection and pointer mapping share those dimensions.

Game loading materializes only the table's grid: every foreground UI/card layer is masked, including player fields, portraits, rail, hand, mana and modal decisions. The actual grid starts teal and blends into its regular gray palette during the 1.1-second zoom. After fullscreen handoff, all foreground UI fades in over 0.65 seconds, with input held until it completes. This is presentation-only and also applies when resuming a match.

The teal grid uses wider, antialiased lines that ease back to the normal one-pixel grid during zoom. The CRT averages a 4×4 sample pattern over each screen pixel's texture footprint during this preview, since live viewport textures have no mipmaps. Materialization reveals those continuous lines without quantizing their texture coordinates or layering a second grid over them; the phosphor arrival sweep remains. Filtering fades out before the fullscreen handoff, and other monitor content keeps its usual treatment.

The initial startup pullback keeps the CRT's physical size, curvature and normal depth testing fixed throughout. A brief screen-space texture projection matches the preceding fullscreen terminal, settling to the glass's fixed UVs before the casing enters view. Only the camera moves outward; the display no longer changes width or floats over the bezel. Pixel and mid-motion checks cover 16:10, 16:9 and ultrawide. The game-to-fullscreen zoom retains its existing expanding-display path.

Home controls and carousel follow the device-local UI size: Automatic uses 100% on Windows and 200% on Mac, with manual 100–200% choices in Settings. At 100%, retain the original Windows header/carousel proportions. Larger scales enlarge the same controls, wrap filters on narrow windows and allow larger disks. Header and carousel dimensions blend during editor navigation; the physical computer and CRT retain their original camera and dimensions. See [display-scaling.md](display-scaling.md).

The editor and battlefield use `ScaledSurface.gd`: a native-resolution SubViewportContainer whose logical coordinates are divided by the selected scale, with engine-managed input conversion. Editor workspace offsets use that same scale. Dialogs can use the full window; the transparent upper region passes input to the carousel/header until a dialog opens. Toolbars wrap and the workspace scrolls at narrow sizes. Game rendering stays in the same viewport during CRT reveal and park/resume, and the surface is freed with the owned match. Verify with `node client-godot/tools/ui-scale-check.mjs` (real isolated library and Commander game).

The PC settings cog uses the same selected scale for its roster, profiles, online controls and dialogs. Its surface stays alive through zoom-in and zoom-out so the CRT image matches the full-window view. Online controls wrap when the logical window is narrow. Editor modals temporarily raise the outer editor surface above carousel disks, then restore its normal layer on dismissal.

Application confirmations (including main-menu Quit) use a separate modal surface at the selected scale above the room, settings and battlefield. It is freed with its dialog so dismissal restores the underlying input.

Drive LEDs keep their emission shader feature enabled and blink by changing emission energy. Toggling the feature during activity causes repeated GL Compatibility shader stalls on macOS. The world uses 2× MSAA to reduce Retina rendering cost; native UI resolution and camera/drive timing are unchanged. The atmosphere shader skips its noise calculations when fully faded.

Physical PLAY and SETTINGS follow the carousel in sibling order so its empty upper input margin does not intercept clicks on the keys. Godot GUI picking follows sibling order rather than the visual `z_index`; keep the keys after the shelf when changing the home layout. Online protocol v2 uses each physical PLAY key to mark that human ready before insertion.
